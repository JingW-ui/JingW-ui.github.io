1. Introduction
1.1 Clinical Background and Significance
The early and accurate diagnosis of primary brain tumors—including meningiomas, gliomas, and pituitary tumors—is paramount for improving patient prognosis and surgical outcomes. As a non-invasive high-resolution imaging modality, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) provides superior soft-tissue contrast essential for intracranial lesion assessment. However, the manual interpretation of multi-sequence MRI volumes is labor-intensive and susceptible to subjective bias. The increasing clinical workload underscores the urgent need for highly efficient, automated Computer-Aided Diagnosis (CAD) tools to assist radiologists in rapid screening and precise localization.
1.2 Challenges in Current AI-Assisted Workflows
While deep learning models, particularly the YOLO (You Only Look Once) framework, have demonstrated remarkable success in object detection, several barriers hinder their seamless integration into clinical radiology:
Deployment Complexity: Most advanced AI pipelines require specialized software environments (e.g., CUDA, complex Python dependencies), which present a high technical barrier for clinical practitioners.
Data Format Gaps: Standard AI models are typically optimized for 2D consumer-grade images, whereas clinical MRI relies on the NIfTI (.nii.gz) format. Bridging the gap between 3D volumetric data and 2D detection architectures often involves inefficient preprocessing.
Computational Constraints: Many high-performance models necessitate expensive GPU hardware. There is a lack of lightweight yet robust solutions capable of performing real-time lesion localization and identification on conventional office-grade hardware (commodity CPUs) without compromising diagnostic accuracy.
1.3 Proposed System and Technical Objectives
To address these limitations, this study presents a high-performance diagnostic GUI developed using YOLO and PySide6, specifically engineered for the clinical environment. The system's primary objective is to provide an end-to-end workflow—from raw data input to automated diagnostic report generation—without requiring any environment configuration.
The core of this work lies in the development of a specialized 3D rapid localization and identification algorithm for .nii.gz data. By optimizing the search logic within the volumetric space, the system avoids the overhead of exhaustive slice-by-slice traversal, ensuring that radiologists can pinpoint lesions with significantly higher efficiency.
1.4 Summary of Key Contributions
1.High-Efficiency Localization Algorithm: We propose a novel strategy for 3D MRI data that achieves an 80% improvement in localization speed compared to standard traversal methods, enabling near-instantaneous lesion identification.
2.Clinical-Grade Usability: The system is delivered as a portable, zero-configuration executable, supporting diverse input sources including static images, real-time video, and 3D NIfTI volumes, thus lowering the entry barrier for non-specialist users.
3.Hardware-Agnostic Performance: The optimized inference engine achieves millisecond-level response times and a 99% detection accuracy on standard commodity CPUs, eliminating the dependency on high-end GPUs for routine clinical use.
4.Integrated Diagnostic Workflow: Beyond detection, the system provides comprehensive features such as batch processing, format conversion, and automated report exporting to support the full clinical decision-making cycle.
