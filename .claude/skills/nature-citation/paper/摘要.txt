Abstract
Objective:​ The clinical translation of deep learning for neuro-oncology is often hindered by high computational demands, complex environment configurations, and a lack of native support for 3D medical imaging formats. This study aims to develop a high-performance, accessible Computer-Aided Diagnosis (CAD) system that enables rapid lesion localization and identification on commodity hardware without requiring specialized technical expertise.
Methods:​ We propose MediScreen-Brain, a lightweight framework built on YOLO and PySide6, distributed as a zero-configuration executable for Windows. To bridge 2D detectors and 3D volumetric data, we formulated an Anatomy-Guided Rapid Localization (AGRL)​ algorithm. AGRL integrates a projection-based hierarchical search strategy that leverages anatomical continuity along the axial axis to constrain the search space in sagittal and coronal views, avoiding exhaustive slice-wise traversal. The system was trained on a public bounding-box dataset and evaluated on a private clinical cohort of 561 isotropic MRI scans, encompassing meningioma, glioma, and pituitary tumors.
Results:​ On standard i7 CPUs, the system achieved 92% detection sensitivity​ for 2D images with millisecond-level inference. For 3D NIfTI volumes, the AGRL algorithm reduced inference cost by 76.8%​ compared to exhaustive search (average processing time: 1.9s vs. 8.2s per volume) while maintaining near-exhaustive detection performance. The platform further integrates automated morphometric measurement (diameter, voxel volume) and structured report generation to streamline clinical workflows.
Conclusion:​ MediScreen-Brain bridges the gap between advanced object detection and practical deployment, democratizing AI-assisted neuro-oncology. Its efficiency on non-specialized hardware makes it a viable solution for both premium and resource-constrained medical facilities. The code and software are available at: https://github.com/JingW-ui/MediScreen-Brain.
